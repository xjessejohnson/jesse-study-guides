About NCE

The National Counselor Examination (NCE) is administered by the NBCC and is a common requirement for professional counselor licensure in many states. It tests knowledge across counseling theories, skills, ethics, assessment, and professional practice.

Typical study time: Most candidates spend 4 to 8 weeks. Those who recently finished a counseling program may need less review; others often benefit from 6–10 weeks of steady practice.

The exam contains 200 multiple-choice items. A common target is roughly 60–66% correct (often cited around 95–105 out of 160 scored items), though exact cut scores vary. Content is weighted across NBCC work-behavior domains and CACREP core areas.

1. Counseling Skills & Interventions 30%
2. Areas of Clinical Focus 29%
3. Professional Practice & Ethics 12%
4. Intake, Assessment & Diagnosis 12%
5. Treatment Planning 9%
6. Core Counseling Attributes 8%

This hub offers full-length practice exams, flashcards, and interactive games to help you review the content areas in an engaging way.

Common roles after NCE (with state licensure): Licensed Professional Counselor (LPC), Mental Health Counselor, Clinical Counselor, Substance Abuse Counselor, School Counselor (where applicable).

Typical US salaries (as of 11/26/2026): BLS median for substance abuse, behavioral disorder, and mental health counselors is about $59,350 (May 2025), with most earning $47k–$77k. Top 10% exceed $97k. Private practice and specialized settings can pay more. Exact figures vary by state, setting, and experience.
